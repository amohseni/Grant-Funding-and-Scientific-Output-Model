## Title candidates (ranked)

1. Funding the capability-resource gap: optimal science funding across multiple rounds
2. Whom to fund, and what peer review is worth: a model of grant allocation over time
3. The price of not knowing: peer review, lotteries, and the capability-resource gap

## Abstract skeleton (Type D template, adapted)

One sentence each: the allocation problem and its two intuitive rules (track record;
under-resourced). Both are suboptimal for the same reason; the optimal rule funds the
capability-resource gap, g* = cK - R (water-filling). The obstacle: the gap is not observable,
and publication counts cannot learn it; the value of peer review is the cost of not knowing the
gap, measured (allocation correlation 0.34 to 0.97 as review sharpens; output gap to the
full-information oracle 17.2 to 1.7 percent of no-funding output). Conditional structure: what
review is worth depends on capability inequality times signal precision; what floors cost is
the targeting value times the floored share; when timing matters requires complementarity
production. One policy consequence (the lottery misprescription) in one sentence.

### 4. The static optimum: fund the gap

- The two intuitive rules refuted by dissociation (grant-and-redirect: track record is right
  about output, wrong about marginal product; need is right about scarcity, wrong about
  capability). Both fail for the same reason: marginal return needs both inputs.
- The rule: g* = cK - R, funding frontier K = R/c, budget sets c. Cite water-filling as
  precedent at the derivation. Figure: optimal grant across the population (report Fig 1).
- Budget conditional: targeting value falls as the budget grows (report Part III). Figure.

### 5. The obstacle: the gap cannot be read off the record

- Publication counts confound capability and resources; the pubs-only funder plateaus at
  allocation correlation 0.13 to 0.18 with the full oracle gap remaining (D-4 baseline).
- Thin grants are capability-uninformative (new mechanism): with g much less than K, output
  pins to the grant; paying for information requires depth (g at least K, budget b at least
  T/2 at the decoupled purse). One quotable policy line for nascent and resource-poor fields.

### 6. Peer review: what a capability signal is worth

- Value = inequality x precision (both necessary; heatmap figure). In heavy-tailed fields a
  coarse signal suffices; light tails need precision.
- Correlation robustness (value survives rho = 0.78, higher under negative correlation).
- Resource signal redundant (D-3, at most 1.1 percent of S1 everywhere): closes the report's
  own open item; the capability signal is the one that matters.
- The empirical anchor made quantitative (D-1): inverting Fang's AUC 0.54 through the model
  gives implied noise tau_K above 20 at heavy and moderate tails, against a value elbow near
  2.5. Real review sits far above the elbow: sharpening review still pays. Corollary stated
  with care: under heavy tails an AUC of 0.54 implies extreme noise, not useless review.
- The overtrust caveat (D-2, stated as the tail-conditional it is): overtrusting a noisy
  signal can turn review's value negative at moderate tails; under heavy tails misweighting
  only attenuates.
- The thesis figure (D-4): allocation converges to the gap rule as review sharpens (corr 0.34
  to 0.97 heavy tail); the oracle output gap shrinks 17.2 to 1.7 percent of S1. The section
  verdict: the value of peer review is the cost of not knowing the gap, now measured.

### 7. The funding horizon (demoted, one section)

- Planning value: small relative to the signal (about 1/30 at the common normalization);
  increasing in epsilon; T-exponent 2.1 to 2.3 for T at most 5, saturating beyond (A5).
  Scope: complementarity production only (Cobb-Douglas kills it; forward reference to 9).
- Mechanism: back-loading, free growth dominating paid growth, now demonstrated by the
  decomposition (pure free back-loads, b_idx to 0.680; pure paid front-loads, 0.481; free
  wins above a ratio of roughly 1/8 to 1/3). The only strict-and-real front-loading is the
  pure-paid corner.
- Subsection: the bootstrap objection at full strength, then corrected. The lump-equivalence
  argument (complementarity licenses positive early spend, not early mass); no front-loading
  regime anywhere (boundary vertical at epsilon* about 0.02, band 0.005 to 0.05; min b_idx
  0.497, economically nil); poverty mutes back-loading, never flips it; depth makes paid
  information work and back-loads the money (seed-and-harvest; discrimination value 0.5 to
  41.2 to 56.8 at b = 0.5, 3, 6; round-1 share 0.110 vs 0.167 even). Figure:
  exploration_depth_schedules.png (the shape is the argument). Verifications in one
  paragraph: planner-free certification; CE self-consistency (honest mispricing caveat
  wherever S8-S5 < 0 appears).

### 8. Floors and lotteries: the price of equality

- The price law (Package A): a floor costs kappa x family targeting value x floored share,
  kappa in [0.10, 0.86]; 6.11 percent of S1 at the focal cell on the strong optimizer; cost
  convex in the floored share; at full flooring it equals the full targeting value (exact).
- Where floors are cheap is exactly where targeting is cheap (ample budget, weak signal,
  light tail). State as a price schedule, never a prohibition (do-not-claim list).
- The misprescription (Type D closing move, proposal for Aydin): the reading "review predicts
  near chance, so replace it with lotteries" backfires precisely in heavy-tailed, tight-budget
  fields, where the price of not targeting is highest and where even a noisy signal separates
  the whales. Lotteries are defensible where the price is lowest; the model prices the choice
  field by field.

### 9. Robustness as results: the production family

- The information story survives the whole CES family including Leontief (signal value
  monotone in inequality and precision everywhere; correlation robustness holds; floors cheap
  in the no-signal family everywhere).
- The Cobb-Douglas boundary: planning value and back-loading vanish at sigma = 1; timing is a
  complementarity phenomenon. Stated as a scoping result, arguably a headline.
- The concentration law (replaces the old conjecture): concentration of optimal funding is
  not monotone in substitutability; it is budget-conditional. Tight budgets concentrate
  toward Leontief; ample budgets equalize (top-up-to-the-bottleneck covers everyone);
  interior maximum at light tails. This resolves what the concentration dispute is about:
  not technology alone, technology times budget. Figure: Gini vs gamma_ces by budget.
- Leontief cells carry the kink-degeneracy caveat (appendix).

### 10. Discussion

- The five-force synthesis: free forces dominate paid; the paid forces regenerate the free
  ones (paid growth resupplies free growth, paid observation resupplies free observation),
  which is why the dominance survives even where the free sources are switched off.
- One quantity prices everything: the dispersion of marginal returns (the value of knowing
  the gap) prices peer review, targeting, and floors alike.
- Empirical calibration agenda: A and K separately identified only by dose-response, never by
  cross-sectional output; what a funder would need to measure; locating real systems on the
  precision curve.
- Limitations as precision: one-dimensional capability is a substantive commitment (E-1b:
  splitting talent variance with a latent productivity factor collapses signal value 69 to
  83 percent; the rule survives observable heterogeneity, E-1a); consumable grants (durable
  residue of a grant is capability, carried by the paid-growth channel); endowments and
  capital-intensive fields fold into the Leontief end.

### 11. Conclusion

- Restate the arc: two rules refuted, the gap rule, the cost of not knowing it, the price
  schedule for equality, the scoping laws. Final sentence bold, bounded, quotable.
