## 3. Main text: structure and word budget (4,000 words)

Introduction, untitled, about 480 words. The PNAS draft in intro-refinement-2026-09-29.md,
section 5, revised for the two wording choices ("how seed grants and lotteries perform
relative to targeted funding"; "concentrated among a few researchers"). Carries the
stakes, the three disputes with citations, why a model, the model in four sentences,
the three results, the two assumptions. The related-literature section does not exist
as a section; its citations move into the first two paragraphs and its content into
the SI text.

Results, about 2,300 words, in four subsections with short bold headings:

1. The optimal allocation funds the capability-resource gap (about 450). The problem
   in words, the display of the gap rule, its reading (targets scale with capability;
   the budget sets the frontier), why each intuitive rule fails, and the budget
   comparative static. Cites Fig 1A-B.
2. What the funder can learn: track records and peer review (about 700). Output
   underdetermines the gap; records improve slowly; thin grants reveal nothing;
   review supplies the signal; the central result on review's value by field, with
   the reasons (the exceptional few are most of the value and easiest to identify);
   review is worth most where money is tight; one sentence each on overtrust and on
   robustness across production functions. Cites Fig 2A-C.
3. When to fund (about 450). The spend-early argument and its failure; the schedule
   leans late once compounding is more than negligible, poverty mutes it; scheduling
   is worth less than the review signal everywhere, and only under complementarity.
   Cites Fig 3A-B.
4. Spreading funds: seed grants, lotteries, and concentration (about 700). What both
   alternatives give up; seed grants (convex loss, sized by targeting's value);
   lotteries (Proposition 2 in one sentence; the five schemes; the shortfall is mostly
   the discarded ranking; the even field inverts the ordering); when lotteries lose
   little; concentration in one sentence with the SI pointer. Cites Fig 4A-B.

Discussion, about 750 words: the key quantity and its two determinants (120); no
mechanism is good or bad in itself, estimate the two features, the two worked cases
(220); peer review implications: effort, the funded-grants reading of the evidence,
calibration (180); limitations (130); the three predictions (100). The conclusion
paragraph of the long version becomes the Discussion's last paragraph (the PNAS
format has no separate conclusion).

Materials and Methods, about 470 words: the model (the production function, the
capability update, resources as baseline plus grant, the budget normalization), the
signals, the strategies (one sentence each; Table S1), the parameter ranges, scoring
and comparison, and the SI and code pointers. Everything the reader needs to know what
was run, with the derivations and the per-figure settings in the SI.

Total about 4,000. Footnotes: none (PNAS uses them only for affiliations); every
number a footnote now carries goes either into the sentence, a figure caption, or the
SI.

## 4. SI Appendix: structure

The SI is the long version, reorganized so that each section is self-contained and
cross-referenced from the main text:

- SI Text 1. Related literature (the long version's S2).
- SI Text 2. The model in full (S3, including Table S1 of strategies and the parameter
  ranges).
- SI Text 3. Derivation of the gap rule (Appendix A, as verified).
- SI Text 4. Lotteries and equal division (Appendix B).
- SI Text 5. Extended results: the material of S4 to S7 that the main text states in
  one sentence (results 12, 15, 16, 19, 24 in full, with Figs S1 to S4: overtrust,
  grant size and schedule, production function, concentration).
- SI Text 6. Robustness to the production function (Appendix C, with Table S2).
- SI Text 7. Simulation specification (Appendix D, with the settings table).
